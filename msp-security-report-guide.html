How to Create MSP Client Security Reports That Actually Get Read

A practical guide for MSPs who want to move from raw vulnerability lists to executive-level reporting that clients understand, act on, and pay for.

TL;DR: Stop sending clients raw vulnerability scans or M365 dashboards. Structure your security reporting around business impact, trends, and actionable next steps. We can help →

Why Most MSP Security Reports Fail

Most MSP security reports are either:

  • Too technical — A raw list of CVEs or Secure Score percentages that the client's CEO can't interpret
  • Too infrequent — A quarterly email with no context, no trend, no call to action
  • Too salesy — Pitching additional services rather than demonstrating value

The result: clients don't read them, don't act on recommendations, and don't see the value of your security stack. The average MSP loses 15-20% of security clients annually due to poor reporting engagement.

The Executive Report Framework

An effective MSP client security report has six components:

SectionWhat it coversLength
1. Executive SummaryWhat changed, what matters, what needs budget½ page
2. Security Score TrendM365 Secure Score, endpoint coverage, patching velocity1 page
3. Vulnerability LandscapeOpen vs resolved, critical/high/medium trends1 page
4. Compliance EvidenceNIS2, GDPR, cyber-insurance controls mapping1 page
5. Incidents & EventsTimeline of notable security events and responses½ page
6. Action RegisterTop 3-5 actions with owner and next review date½ page

1. The Executive Summary

This is the only section most executives will read. Make it count:

  • One sentence on the overall security posture trend (improving/stable/declining)
  • One sentence on the most critical finding this period
  • One sentence on what needs budget or management attention next quarter

"Your M365 Secure Score improved 12 points to 78/100 this quarter. Three critical vulnerabilities were remediated within SLA, but two new high-severity findings need budget approval for the licensing required to patch them. We recommend approving the Defender for Identity upgrade (DKK 8,400/year) before the next audit cycle."

2. Security Score Trend

Use a simple month-over-month table or chart showing:

  • M365 Secure Score — Overall score + category breakdown (Identity, Device, Data, Apps, Infrastructure)
  • Endpoint coverage — % of devices with active EDR, patched within 30 days
  • Patching velocity — Mean time to patch critical and high vulnerabilities

3. Vulnerability Landscape

Don't dump a raw scan. Summarize:

  • Trend: Open vs resolved this period vs last period
  • Severity distribution: Critical, high, medium, low counts
  • Top 3 findings: Most impactful vulnerabilities with business context
  • Aging: Vulnerabilities past SLA with owner and risk note

4. Compliance Evidence Mapping (NIS2, GDPR, Cyber-Insurance)

Map your existing controls to the relevant frameworks:

  • NIS2 Article 21 — Risk analysis, incident handling, business continuity, supply chain security
  • Cyber-insurance questionnaires — MFA coverage, backup frequency, incident response plan, access controls
  • GDPR Article 32 — Security of processing, pseudonymization, encryption, resilience

Each control should show: Status (Implemented / Partial / Missing) + Evidence reference + Next action

5. Incidents & Events Timeline

List all notable security events in the period:

  • Date/time, event type, severity, detection method
  • Response actions taken and outcome
  • Current status (closed, monitoring, escalated)

6. Action Register

End with 3-5 concrete actions ranked by business impact:

  • Action — What needs to be done
  • Priority — Critical / High / Medium
  • Owner — Client, MSP, or shared
  • Target date — When it should be completed
  • Business impact — What happens if not done

Making It Repeatable

The biggest mistake MSPs make is building each report from scratch. You need a repeatable template pipeline:

  • A JSON schema that defines the data structure for each report section
  • A local generator that transforms the JSON into formatted Markdown/HTML
  • Branding templates for client-specific logos and color schemes
  • A review-and-approve workflow before the report goes to the client

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • No trend data: One-off reports have no context. Always compare to the previous period.
  • No business translation: "12 critical vulnerabilities" means nothing. "Your external RDP exposure creates a ransomware risk that could halt operations for 3-5 days" is actionable.
  • No ownership: Every finding needs an owner, or nothing gets done.
  • Too long: If the executive summary doesn't fit on one page, the full report won't be read.
  • Inconsistent formatting: Different formats every month erodes trust and readability.
